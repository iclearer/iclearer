How to clear cache on Mac

Updated 12 November 2026 · By the iClearer team

Short answer

To clear app caches on a Mac, quit your apps, open Finder, press Shift Command G, go to ~/Library/Caches, move the contents of the folders you want to clear to the Trash, and empty the Trash. Browser caches are cleared from inside each browser. Deleting caches is safe: apps rebuild them, though the first launch afterwards can be a little slower.

The kinds of cache on a Mac

CacheWhere it livesSafe to clear?
App (user) cache~/Library/CachesYes. Apps rebuild it.
Browser cacheEach browser’s own folder, mostly under ~/Library/CachesYes. Pages reload once.
System cache/Library/CachesUsually. macOS rebuilds it.
Developer caches~/.npm, ~/Library/Caches/Homebrew, Xcode DerivedDataYes. Tools download or rebuild.

Clear app caches

  1. Quit the apps whose cache you want to clear.
  2. In Finder, press Shift Command G.
  3. Type ~/Library/Caches and press Return.
  4. Press Command J and tick Calculate All Sizes to see which folders are big.
  5. Open a large folder and move its contents to the Trash. Keeping the folder itself avoids permission problems.
  6. Empty the Trash to get the space back.

Clear browser caches

Safari

  1. Open Safari → Settings → Advanced and tick Show features for web developers. On older versions it is called Show Develop menu in menu bar.
  2. Choose Develop → Empty Caches, or press Option Command E.

Chrome

  1. Press Shift Command Delete.
  2. Tick Cached images and files, choose a time range and confirm.

Firefox

  1. Open Firefox → Settings → Privacy & Security.
  2. Under Cookies and Site Data, click Clear Data, tick the cached files option and confirm.

Note: clearing the cache does not sign you out. Clearing cookies does, so leave that box unticked if you want to stay logged in.

Clear system caches

System caches live in /Library/Caches (no ~ at the start). macOS and shared components rebuild them, and they are usually small. You will be asked for your password to remove them. Leave /System/Library/Caches alone, because macOS protects and manages it.

Is it safe, and how often should you do it?

Clearing caches is safe. The trade-off is speed: caches exist so apps do not have to download or rebuild the same data, so the first launch after clearing can be slower and the cache will grow back.

That is why you do not need to clear caches on a schedule. Do it when you are short on space, or when one app is misbehaving and a fresh cache might fix it.

Frequently asked questions

Is it safe to delete everything in ~/Library/Caches?

Yes. Everything in ~/Library/Caches is data apps can recreate. Quit your apps first, and delete the contents of the folders rather than the folders themselves. Apps may open more slowly the first time afterwards.

Will clearing the cache delete my passwords or log me out?

No. Caches hold temporary copies of data, not passwords. Passwords are stored in Keychain. You are only signed out of websites if you also clear cookies.

Does clearing the cache make a Mac faster?

Not usually. It frees disk space and can fix an app that is behaving oddly, but caches exist to make apps faster, so apps can feel slower until the cache rebuilds. A Mac with a nearly full disk does run better once space is freed.

How often should I clear the cache on my Mac?

Only when you need the space or an app is misbehaving. There is no benefit to clearing caches on a fixed schedule, because they grow back as you use your apps.

Where are cache files stored on a Mac?

App caches are in ~/Library/Caches, system caches are in /Library/Caches, and browsers keep theirs in their own folders, mostly under ~/Library/Caches. In Finder, press Shift-Command-G and type the path to open any of them.

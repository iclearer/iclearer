How to clean up your Mac: a 10-step checklist

Updated 12 November 2026 · By the iClearer team

Short answer

To clean up a Mac, work from the safest step to the most involved: empty the Trash, clear the Downloads folder, uninstall apps you do not use, clear app caches and logs, delete large files you no longer need, and remove old iPhone backups. Check System Settings → General → Storage before and after to see how much space you got back. Everything on this list can be done for free with what is built into macOS.

Steps 1–7: get space back

1. Check what is using your storage

Open the Apple menu → System Settings → General → Storage. Wait for the bar to finish calculating and note the free space, so you can compare afterwards. Click the i next to a category to see what is in it.

2. Empty the Trash

Files in the Trash still use space. Right-click the Trash in the Dock and choose Empty Trash. To have macOS do this for you, turn on Empty Trash Automatically in Storage settings; it removes items that have been in the Trash for 30 days.

3. Clear the Downloads folder

Open Downloads in Finder, switch to List view and click the Size column. Installers (.dmg, .pkg) and .zip files you already opened are usually safe to delete.

4. Uninstall apps you do not use

Open Finder → Applications, switch to List view and sort by Date Last Opened. Drag apps you no longer use to the Trash. Their support files stay behind in your Library folder; how to uninstall apps on Mac completely shows where.

5. Clear app caches and logs

Quit your apps, then in Finder press Shift Command G, go to ~/Library/Caches and move the contents of the largest folders to the Trash. Do the same in ~/Library/Logs. Apps rebuild caches when they need them. Step-by-step: how to clear cache on Mac.

6. Delete large and old files

In Storage settings, click the i next to Documents and open Large Files. Old videos, disk images and virtual machines are the usual finds. More methods: how to find large files on Mac.

7. Remove old iPhone and iPad backups

In Storage settings, click the i next to iOS Files, select a backup you no longer need and click Delete. Keep the most recent backup of any device you still use. More detail: how to delete old iPhone backups on Mac.

Steps 8–10: keep it tidy

8. Trim Messages attachments

Photos and videos sent in Messages are stored on your Mac. In Storage settings, click the i next to Messages to see the largest attachments and delete the ones you do not need.

9. Review what opens at login

Open System Settings → General → Login Items (called Login Items & Extensions on newer versions of macOS). Remove apps you do not need at startup. For background items, check which app each one belongs to before you switch it off. This does not free disk space, but your Mac starts faster.

10. Restart and update macOS

A restart clears temporary files and swap files. Then check System Settings → General → Software Update; after an update installs, macOS removes the installer it downloaded.

Will cleaning make my Mac faster?

It depends on why it is slow. If the disk is nearly full, yes: macOS needs free space for swap files and updates, and a Mac with only a few gigabytes free can slow down noticeably. If you have plenty of free space, deleting caches will not speed anything up, because caches exist to make apps faster.

What you should not delete

  • Anything in /System or /usr. That is macOS.
  • ~/Library/Keychains, where your passwords are stored.
  • ~/Library/Application Support folders for apps you still use. They hold your data, not cache.
  • The only backup of a device you still use.

Frequently asked questions

How do I clean my Mac for free?

Use what is built into macOS. Open System Settings → General → Storage, empty the Trash, clear the Downloads folder, delete large files from the Documents list, and clear the folders in ~/Library/Caches. None of this needs extra software.

Does a Mac have a built-in cleaner?

Not a one-click cleaner. macOS has Storage settings (System Settings → General → Storage), which shows what is using space, lists large files and offers recommendations such as Empty Trash Automatically and Optimize Storage. It does not clear app caches or remove leftovers from deleted apps.

How often should I clean up my Mac?

When free space gets low, not on a schedule. A sensible trigger is when less than 10–15% of the disk is free, or when macOS warns that the disk is almost full.

Will cleaning my Mac make it faster?

Only if the disk was nearly full. macOS needs free space for swap files and updates, so freeing space on a full disk helps. On a Mac with plenty of free space, clearing caches does not improve speed.

What is the safest thing to delete first on a Mac?

The Trash, then old installers and zip files in Downloads, then app caches in ~/Library/Caches. These are either files you already chose to delete or files your apps can recreate.

How to uninstall apps on Mac completely

Updated 12 November 2026 · By the iClearer team

Short answer

To uninstall an app on a Mac, open Finder → Applications, drag the app to the Trash and empty the Trash. That removes the app itself but leaves its support files behind in your Library folder. To remove an app completely, also delete its folders in ~/Library/Application Support, ~/Library/Caches, ~/Library/Containers and its file in ~/Library/Preferences.

1. Remove the app

  1. Quit the app. If it will not quit, press Option Command Esc and force quit it.
  2. Open Finder → Applications.
  3. Drag the app to the Trash, or select it and press Command Delete.
  4. Empty the Trash.

2. Where leftovers live

Apps store their data outside the app itself, and macOS does not remove it when the app goes. These are the usual places:

FolderWhat is in it
~/Library/Application SupportApp data, often the largest leftover
~/Library/CachesCached files
~/Library/Containers and ~/Library/Group ContainersData for sandboxed and App Store apps
~/Library/PreferencesSettings files ending in .plist
~/Library/LogsLog files
~/Library/Saved Application StateWindow positions from the last session
~/Library/LaunchAgentsHelpers that start when you log in

3. Find an app’s leftover files

Leftovers are named after the app or after its bundle identifier, which looks like com.company.appname. Before you delete the app, get its identifier in Terminal (replace Slack with the app’s name):

osascript -e 'id of app "Slack"'

Then, in Finder, press Shift Command G, open each folder in the table above, and look for the app’s name or identifier. Move what you find to the Trash.

Check the name twice. Several apps from the same company can share a folder, so make sure a folder belongs only to the app you removed.

Apps with their own uninstaller

Some apps install drivers, system extensions or background services. These usually ship an uninstaller, either inside the app’s folder in Applications or as a download from the developer. Use it, because it removes parts that dragging to the Trash cannot.

Apps that came with macOS, such as Safari and Mail, are protected by the system and cannot be removed.

Frequently asked questions

Does dragging an app to the Trash uninstall it completely?

No. It removes the app itself, but support files, caches, preferences and containers stay in your Library folder. They can take up more space than the app did.

Where are leftover app files stored on a Mac?

Mostly in ~/Library/Application Support, ~/Library/Caches, ~/Library/Containers, ~/Library/Preferences and ~/Library/Logs. They are named after the app or its bundle identifier, such as com.company.appname.

How do I uninstall an app that will not delete?

The app is probably still running. Quit it, or press Option-Command-Esc to force quit it, then try again. If macOS asks for a password, you need an administrator account to remove it.

Can I delete the apps that came with my Mac?

Not the ones that are part of macOS, such as Safari, Mail and Messages. They are protected by the system and take no extra space you can reclaim.

Is it safe to delete leftover files from uninstalled apps?

Yes, as long as the folder belongs only to the app you removed. Deleting leftovers also deletes that app’s settings and saved data, so keep them if you plan to reinstall the app and want your settings back.
